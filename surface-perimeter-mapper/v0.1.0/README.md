# 3D Surface Perimeter Mapper (initial cylinder scan)

An independent gSender plugin for guided probing with a 3D touch probe. This build adds an automated perimeter scan for a simple round cylinder centered within the entered part dimensions. It does not yet trace arbitrary convex or concave outlines.

## Before scanning

1. Enter probe tip diameter, usable thin-shaft length, vertical allowance, and approach clearance.
2. Enter minimum and maximum part thickness. The plugin calculates the perimeter probe height and a full-clearance height of `0.75 × tip diameter + (maximum thickness − minimum thickness)` above WPOS Z0.
3. Enter the approximate **radius R** of the circle, set perimeter sample spacing, and choose **Fast** or **Precise** probing. Maximum spacing is calculated as one quarter of the smaller part dimension so the scan keeps at least four samples across it (for R = 31.5 mm, the maximum is 15.75 mm). This limit is based on part size, not probe-tip diameter. Smaller spacing is allowed, subject to the 360-point and interior-sample safety limits. The search rectangle is calculated from the resulting diameter plus the configured margin. Fast records one touch per point; Precise uses the existing fast touch plus a slower confirming touch.
4. Home, jog to the lower-left search-rectangle corner, and press **Probe WPOS Origin**. Complete the clear-trigger-clear probe-signal check. The plugin double-touches the bed, sets G54 WPOS X0 Y0 Z0, and retracts to the required clearance height.
5. Review the calculated sample count and press **Find bed and scan surface**. Confirm the motion warning and remain beside the machine. After finding the bed, the probe retracts directly to the calculated perimeter height and begins the first ray from that same position. Use **Stop after current move** to stop at the next safe retract point.

## How the cylinder scan works

- It samples radial directions around the approximate part center, starting at the front edge and moving around one revolution. The first ray searches inward to `R − 5 mm` from the center to allow for an approximate origin. From sample three onward, each next contact radius is linearly predicted from the last five measured radii.
- Each radial search is now one continuous linear `G38.3` move. The controller stops the move on probe contact; the plugin then reads the contact position and retracts outward to confirm the input clears. Later rays can search inward by up to `R` from the predicted ball-center contact radius, giving the probe room to enter radial bays and find an inward wall. The ray stops just before the center; a no-contact result stops the scan, and the diagnostic log records each search range.
- Fast mode records the first touch; Precise mode confirms it with a slower second touch. After each touch, the plugin moves radially outward by 0.75 × the tip diameter and requires the probe input to clear. Between angular points it moves laterally at the perimeter probing height without raising Z. Z is raised for the initial approach, completion, or recovery.
- It checks the planned WPOS positions against machine travel derived from `$23`, `$130`, `$131`, and `$132`. It requires active G54, a homed and idle controller, and a tested, clear `Pn:P` input.
- Captured ball-center contacts are corrected inward by the entered tip radius. Use **Download perimeter CSV** to inspect the resulting edge coordinates.
- After the perimeter completes, the probe rises to the calculated full-clearance height and samples the top surface in nested 360-degree rings. The first ring is offset inward by `0.75 × tip diameter`; subsequent rings move inward by the configured perimeter sample spacing. A center point closes the surface mesh. Each interior Z probe travels down to the measured bed height. If the probe does not trigger, the plugin retries that point once, then pauses at full clearance for the operator to check the probe and continue (retrying the same point) or cancel. Precise mode adds a slower confirming touch for part-surface contacts; it skips the second downward touch when the first contact is at bed level.
- Before interior motion starts, all planned ring points are checked against reported machine travel. The scan stops if the plan exceeds 12,000 interior Z samples. The result includes an isometric surface view with adjustable vertical exaggeration, perimeter CSV, and copyable ASCII STL. STL XY is relative to WPOS origin and Z is relative to the first interior reading; the STL is an open top-surface mesh without a bottom or side walls.

This is a first machine-test implementation. The entered radius and WPOS origin are approximate; the first inward ray allows a 5 mm center offset. Later radial rays can enter bays up to one entered radius, but this does not guarantee safe or complete tracing of arbitrary concave outlines, especially where a bay has multiple walls along a ray. The part must be stable and free of clamps or obstacles along every radial approach. Surface rings assume the captured perimeter can be represented around WPOS X0 Y0. Machine travel checking cannot detect fixtures, cable snags, a loose part, or a misconfigured probe. Keep the spindle off, stay beside the machine, and supervise the complete perimeter and interior scan. Controller soft limits remain the final motion boundary. Build/package checks do not validate live CNC motion; the new inward search needs supervised machine testing.
